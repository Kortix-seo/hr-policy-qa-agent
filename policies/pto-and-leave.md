# Time off and leave

This file is example policy text for the reference project in this repository: an open-source HR policy helpdesk agent that runs on Kortix, the open-source AI Operating System. The company behind these numbers is invented, and so is every balance and deadline below. They exist so the agent has a specific sentence to quote, and so a reader can see what a policy file has to contain before an agent may answer from it. Nothing here describes Kortix's own employment practice. Open source. Read it, fork it, audit it.

A real company replaces the numbers with its own and keeps the shape: a heading per question, a rule with a number in it, and no place where the reader has to guess.

## Paid time off

Full-time employees get 25 days of paid time off each calendar year, accrued at a little over two days a month. Part-time employees accrue the same 25 days pro rata. Time off covers vacation, personal days and religious observance. Public holidays are separate and do not come out of the 25 days.

## Carry over into the next year

Up to 15 days of unused time off carry over into the next year. Anything above 15 days is forfeited on 31 December. Carried days expire on 31 March if they are still unused, and they are not paid out instead. A request to carry more than 15 days is an exception, and this policy does not grant it.

## Requesting time off

Submit the request in the HR system at least 10 working days before the first day off. A manager approves or declines within three working days. A request for a week or longer, and any request that overlaps a blackout period the team has published, needs the approval before anyone books travel.

## Sick leave

Employees get 10 paid sick days a year. Notify your manager the same morning, before the working day starts, by message or in the HR system. A note is not required for an absence under three days. From the fourth consecutive day, upload a doctor's note to the HR system. Sick leave does not come out of the 25 days of paid time off, and it does not need to be planned in advance.

## Parental leave

Parental leave is 20 weeks for a birth, an adoption or a surrogacy arrangement, for the parent who is the primary caregiver. It can start up to four weeks before the expected date.

To request it, write to your manager and to HR at least eight weeks before the intended start date, and give the expected date or the placement date. HR confirms the dates, the pay treatment and the return date in writing within five working days. A request that arrives with less than eight weeks' notice is still accepted. The notice period exists so that cover can be arranged, not as a condition of the leave.

## Bereavement leave

Five paid days for a parent, child, sibling or partner. Two paid days for a grandparent, grandchild, aunt, uncle, cousin or in-law. Travel days are not counted against the allowance when the funeral is more than 300 miles away. Tell your manager as soon as you know, and take the days in whatever order the situation allows.

## Public holidays

The company observes the public holidays of the country named in the employment contract, published each December for the year ahead. A public holiday that falls on a Saturday or Sunday moves to the nearest working day. An employee who works on a public holiday takes a replacement day within 60 days.

## What this file does not answer

The rules above are general. This file does not answer a question about a medical condition, a disability, a pregnancy complication or one person's family situation, and it does not grant an exception to any of its own numbers. Questions of that kind go to an HR person through the bar in `escalation_rules.yaml`, which records the question and the rule id and returns no draft.
